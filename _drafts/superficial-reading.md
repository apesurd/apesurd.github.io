title: Superficial Reading
excerpt: Reading difficult books without getting discouraged 
---

## Definition
It's a method for getting a quick grasp of a difficult text without deep comprehension on the first pass.

Emphasizes that great books aren’t meant to be fully understood on the first read; comparing this to the Bible, which scholars study for decades.

Encourages reframing expectations: even a small increment of understanding (1–10%) from the first read is valuable progress.

Strategy: Read a difficult book from start to finish without stopping, ignoring footprints, commentaries, and unknown terms.

Shift focus to what you do understand - those "sweet spots" - cherish incremental gains over paralysis from confusion.

Getting stuck on the minor details early on undermines comprehension of the broader story or argument.

Ignore footprints, and commentary, press through difficulty, and celebrate learning something, no matter how small.

Superficial reading builds both reading stamina and wisdom, and that future rereads with yield deeper insights as you grow.
